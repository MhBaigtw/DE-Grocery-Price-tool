2. What the comparison cannot see

Store brands, as a share of each chain's price-weighted shelf presence.

A store brand has one seller, so it has no barcode shared with a competitor and can never enter a barcode-matched basket. This is the size of that blind spot.

3. How often products are on sale

Within each chain only. These bars must not be compared against each other.

Share of observed days on which the median product carries a struck-out price.

The cross-chain comparison is unavailable, and that is a finding. A struck-out price does not mean the same thing at every chain. Where a product has one, independent promotional text confirms it on … of rows at one chain and … at another — and at Metro the check is impossible, because its promotional-text field is empty on every row. Why there is no “cheapest basket” tool here

A shopper typing a list and getting “Walmart is cheapest” is precisely the claim this analysis does not support. The comparison above covers identical national-brand products and is blind to roughly a quarter of the shelf; for about half the individual products in it, neither chain is reliably cheaper. A lookup tool would hide both facts behind a single answer, so it was not built.
